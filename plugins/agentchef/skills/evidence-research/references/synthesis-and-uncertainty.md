# Synthesis and Uncertainty

## Claim ledger

Every material claim needs:

- stable claim ID;
- concise statement;
- `fact`, `inference`, or `recommendation`;
- source references;
- confidence;
- uncertainty and applicability notes;
- relevant counterevidence or disagreement.

Facts describe checked evidence. Inferences explain what the evidence probably
means. Recommendations add judgment about what to do. Keep them visibly
separate.

## Synthesis

1. Group evidence by sub-question, mechanism, theme, option, or outcome.
2. Compare direction, magnitude, context, and method rather than counting
   sources as equal votes.
3. Explain disagreement through population, definitions, incentives, dates,
   data quality, methods, or genuinely unresolved evidence.
4. Identify missing perspectives and negative or null evidence.
5. Translate findings into decision options, prerequisites, risks, and
   reversible next steps.

Use meta-analysis only when studies, outcomes, and designs are sufficiently
compatible and the statistical assumptions can be defended. Otherwise use a
transparent narrative, tabular, thematic, or framework synthesis.

## Uncertainty language

Prefer:

- "The checked evidence supports..."
- "This is consistent with..."
- "A reasonable inference is..."
- "Evidence is limited because..."
- "The recommendation depends on..."

Avoid "proves", "always", "guaranteed", or false precision unless the source and
method genuinely support that level of certainty.
