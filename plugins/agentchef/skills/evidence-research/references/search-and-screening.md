# Search and Screening

## Search design

Break the question into concepts. For each concept record:

- preferred term, synonyms, acronyms, and historical labels;
- controlled vocabulary where the database supports it;
- entities, geographies, populations, outcomes, and comparators;
- date, language, document-type, and jurisdiction limits;
- primary databases, official portals, registries, standards bodies, and
  company or regulatory sources.

Pilot the query, inspect known relevant records, then adjust. Preserve the exact
query, system, filters, result date, and result count when available. Search
engine snippets are discovery aids, not final evidence.

## Retrieval order

1. official laws, standards, regulatory materials, datasets, filings, and
   first-party technical documentation;
2. original studies, trials, surveys, datasets, and methods papers;
3. systematic reviews or high-quality syntheses;
4. expert or analyst interpretation for context;
5. news or community discussion for leads and lived experience, with lower
   evidentiary weight.

The appropriate hierarchy depends on the question. A user-experience question
may require primary interviews; a legal claim requires current primary law and
jurisdiction; a product API claim requires official documentation.

## Screening

- Deduplicate identifiers, titles, and versions.
- Apply declared inclusion and exclusion rules consistently.
- For structured or systematic work, record title/abstract and full-text
  decisions and reasons for exclusion.
- Keep preprints, retractions, corrections, protocol papers, and final reports
  distinguishable.
- When full text is unavailable, label the source as abstract-only or
  metadata-only and reduce the claim accordingly.

## Stopping

Stop when the charter’s source classes are covered, citation chaining no longer
changes decision-relevant themes, major disagreements are represented, and the
remaining uncertainty is explicit. Do not claim exhaustive searching unless
the protocol and databases justify it.
