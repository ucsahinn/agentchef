# Scoping and Charter

## Start with the decision

A research question is useful only when its answer changes a decision. Record:

- decision owner and audience;
- primary question and three to five focused sub-questions;
- population, product, market, intervention, outcome, or comparison entities;
- geography, languages, and time horizon;
- deadline, source-access limits, and acceptable uncertainty;
- explicit exclusions and stopping rule;
- final artifact and review cycle.

## Scope modes

| Mode | Best for | Minimum output |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Fast understanding | Key sources, findings, gaps, next research |
| Decision support | Options and trade-offs | Evidence matrix, confidence, recommendation |
| Landscape | Players, trends, gaps | Entity map, comparison basis, uncertainty |
| Implementation | Execution requirements | Preconditions, risks, sequence, validation |
| Evidence review | Body of studies | Protocol, search, screening, appraisal, synthesis |
| Original analysis | New data or model | Provenance, methods, code, sensitivity, ethics |

Do not promise fixed pages, hours, or calendar time without considering topic
complexity, access, languages, data volume, and review rigor. If an estimate is
useful, label it as a planning estimate and list its assumptions.

## Default when inputs are incomplete

Use a bounded decision-support scan:

- one primary question;
- up to five sub-questions;
- recent official and original sources first, plus foundational sources;
- explicit search log and stopping rule;
- narrative synthesis with confidence and uncertainty;
- report plus source and claim appendix.

Expand to interviews, surveys, paid data, or modeling only when the desk
evidence leaves a decision-critical gap and the user authorizes the expansion.
