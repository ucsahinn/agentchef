# Content and Growth

## Intent and page mapping

Map topics to the user job and suitable page type:

- informational: explanation, guide, documentation, or problem solving;
- commercial investigation: comparison, alternatives, use case, or category;
- transactional: product, pricing, signup, or conversion page;
- navigational: brand, login, support, or documentation destination.

Avoid one-page-per-keyword duplication. Consolidate overlapping intents into a
strong canonical page and use supporting content where it adds distinct value.
Volumes, difficulty scores, competitor traffic, and conversion estimates must
name their source and collection date; otherwise label them unknown.

## People-first quality

Evaluate:

- who created or reviewed the content and whether that identity is accurate;
- what first-hand experience, method, evidence, or product proof supports it;
- why the content exists and whether it solves the stated user task;
- whether claims, dates, comparisons, and screenshots are current;
- whether the page provides a useful next step without hiding the answer.

E-E-A-T is a lens for trust and quality, especially in high-risk topics. It is
not a score to fabricate or a standalone ranking switch.

## Programmatic and AI-assisted content

Automation is not automatically prohibited. The risk is scaled content that
exists mainly to manipulate rankings and adds little original value. Require a
real editorial purpose, source traceability, template-level quality controls,
human review proportional to risk, duplicate detection, and a sustainable
update or retirement path.

## Comparisons and alternatives

Use verifiable criteria, current dates, transparent methodology, and direct
product evidence. Do not invent competitor weaknesses, fake neutrality, or
publish large interchangeable comparison sets. Legal or brand-risk review may
be required before publication.

## Authority and promotion

Prefer useful original research, legitimate partnerships, public
documentation, customer-approved case studies, and editorially earned
references. Link buying, private networks, abusive guest-post scale, expired
domain manipulation, and site-reputation exploitation are out of bounds.
External outreach and publication require approval.
