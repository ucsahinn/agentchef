# Qualitative and Quantitative Methods

## Qualitative

Use qualitative methods for experience, process, implementation, meaning,
barriers, or stakeholder perspective:

- purposeful or stratified sampling tied to the question;
- semi-structured interviews, documents, observations, or open responses;
- consent and data-minimization plan before collection;
- codebook with definitions and examples;
- thematic, framework, content, or comparative-case analysis;
- negative-case search, reflexive notes, and participant or expert validation
  when appropriate.

Do not invent saturation, interviews, quotes, or coding agreement.

## Quantitative

Before modeling:

1. preserve raw data and provenance;
2. document units, joins, exclusions, and derived fields;
3. profile duplicates, missingness, distributions, outliers, and time coverage;
4. inspect selection effects and measurement changes;
5. define outcomes and comparisons before testing.

Use descriptive statistics first. Add inference, regression, classification,
clustering, forecasting, or causal methods only when the design, sample, and
assumptions support them. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not p-values
alone. Use sensitivity analysis for consequential assumptions.

## Mixed methods

Use mixed methods when qualitative context and quantitative patterns answer
complementary parts of the same question. State where they connect: sampling,
instrument design, explanation, triangulation, or integration. Do not place two
methods side by side without an integration plan.

## Reproducible tooling

Use a versioned notebook or script, data dictionary, environment notes, and
deterministic outputs where possible. Add packages only after approval. Never
embed credentials or restricted raw data in code or deliverables.
