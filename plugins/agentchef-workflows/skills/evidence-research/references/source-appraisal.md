# Source Appraisal

Evaluate each source on the dimensions that matter for the question:

- provenance and proximity to the data-generating event;
- author, institution, funder, conflicts, and editorial process;
- method, design, sampling, measurement, and comparison;
- population or corpus, geography, period, and applicability;
- missingness, attrition, confounding, bias, and uncertainty;
- version, update date, correction, retraction, or supersession risk;
- whether the conclusion exceeds the reported results.

## Confidence labels

- `high`: directly relevant primary or authoritative evidence with strong
  methods and corroboration.
- `medium`: useful and reasonably supported, with material limitations or
  indirectness.
- `low`: limited, conflicting, old, weakly applicable, or methodologically
  fragile.
- `context-only`: useful for framing or discovery, not sufficient to support a
  decision claim alone.

Confidence is claim-specific. A strong paper can support one claim well and
another poorly.

## Source matrix

Capture:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| ID and citation | Stable claim reference |
| Source type and URL/identifier | Provenance |
| Checked date and version | Freshness |
| Question fit | Relevance |
| Method and sample/corpus | Validity |
| Findings | What the source actually reports |
| Limitations and conflicts | Risk |
| Confidence and outdated risk | Weight |
| Supported claim IDs | Traceability |

Do not copy a source’s authority label into the conclusion. Appraise what the
source can actually establish.
